# RFC 9002 — QUIC Loss Detection and Congestion Control

Details of the rows of the [RFC compliance summary](../rfc-compliance.md) for this document. Each section gives the requirement, how Kuicksilver meets it (with the files), the tests that pin it, its history, notes on peers and the open items. Status: ✅ met · 🔶 feature subset · ⬜ planned · ❌ not planned; "since" is the release from which the current behaviour holds (history below and in [CHANGELOG.md](../../../CHANGELOG.md)).

Loss detection and the PTO live in `kuicksilver-quic/src/commonMain/kotlin/io/kuicksilver/quic/recovery/` (`LossDetection.kt`, `RttEstimator.kt`, `PacketNumberSpace.kt`, `Pacer.kt`); the controllers are `NewReno.kt`, `Cubic.kt` (the default, [RFC 9438](9438.md#cubic)) and the experimental `Bbr.kt` ([draft-ietf-ccwg-bbr](draft-ietf-ccwg-bbr.md#bbr-bbrv3)) behind `CongestionController`; the core applies them in `core/PacketBuilder.kt` (window, pacer, probes), `core/ConnectionCore.timers.kt` (probes, the loss listener) and `core/ConnectionCore.deadlines.kt` (timers). Every congestion requirement below holds for all three controllers unless a row says otherwise. Until 0.3.0 this file was one summary row; the 0.3.0 RFC review split it by section and fixed what the rows below record under History.

## <a id="s5-rtt-estimation"></a>§5.1–§5.3 — RTT samples, min_rtt, smoothed_rtt and rttvar

Status: ✅ since 0.3.0 · [summary](../rfc-compliance.md#quic)

Original row: RFC 9002 (loss detection, PTO, NewReno), split in 0.3.0.

**Requirement.** §5.1: an RTT sample only when the largest acknowledged packet is newly acknowledged and ack-eliciting. §5.2: min_rtt is the first sample, then the minimum, never adjusted for ack delay; "Endpoints SHOULD set the min_rtt to the newest RTT sample after persistent congestion is established". §5.3: the first sample sets smoothed_rtt and rttvar = sample / 2; the endpoint "SHOULD ignore the peer's max_ack_delay until the handshake is confirmed", "MUST use the lesser of the acknowledgment delay and the peer's max_ack_delay after the handshake is confirmed", "MUST NOT subtract the acknowledgment delay from the RTT sample if the resulting value is smaller than the min_rtt", and "SHOULD subtract such local delays [acknowledgments whose keys were not yet available] from its RTT sample until the handshake is confirmed".

**Recorded status.** ✅ 0.2.0 (the local-delay SHOULD since 0.3.0)

**How it is met.**

- `LossDetection.onAck` samples only when the largest newly acknowledged packet is the frame's Largest Acknowledged and ack-eliciting, and only for packets sent on the active path (each path has its own `RttEstimator`, RFC 9000 §9.4)
- the ack delay is decoded with the peer's ack_delay_exponent and saturates instead of wrapping on a peer-chosen value
- `RttEstimator.onSample`: first sample sets min_rtt, smoothed_rtt and rttvar = sample / 2; then min_rtt = min(min_rtt, sample) without ack delay; the ack delay is capped at max_ack_delay only once the handshake is confirmed, and subtracted only when that does not take the sample below min_rtt; rttvar = 3/4 rttvar + 1/4 |smoothed − adjusted|, smoothed = 7/8 smoothed + 1/8 adjusted
- after persistent congestion min_rtt restarts from the newest sample (`RttEstimator.onPersistentCongestion`)
- a packet held for its keys (`PacketReceiver` keeps its arrival time with it) is processed later, but an ACK in it is sampled from its arrival (`LossDetection.onAck`'s `arrivedAt`): the wait for keys is a local delay. Packets wait for keys only before the handshake is confirmed, so the sample is exact afterwards too; an ACK claiming a packet sent after that arrival (only a guessing peer can) is sampled at the current time
- MAYs not taken: the ack delay of Initial ACKs is applied like any other (§5.3 "MAY ignore"; peers send 0 there), and samples are not dropped before confirmation when the adjusted value falls below min_rtt (§5.3 "MAY ignore RTT samples"; the MUST NOT above already keeps them from lowering the estimate)

**Tests that pin it.**

- `recovery.RttEstimatorTest.*` (`initialValues`, `firstSample100ms`, `secondSampleWithAckDelay`, `ackDelayNotClampedBeforeHandshakeConfirmed`, `ackDelayClampedToMaxAckDelay`, `adjustedNeverBelowMinRtt`)
- `recovery.LossDetectionTest.hugeAckDelayDoesNotOverflow`, `.persistentCongestionResetsMinRttToTheNewestSample`
- `core.BufferedAckRttTest.anAckHeldForItsKeysIsSampledAtItsArrival`

**History.**

- 0.3.0 RFC review: an ACK in a packet held for its keys (a server's 1-RTT packet before the client's Finished is verified) was sampled when the packet was fed again, so its RTT included the wait

## <a id="s6-1-ack-based-loss-detection"></a>§6.1 — acknowledgment-based loss detection

Status: ✅ since 0.2.0 · [summary](../rfc-compliance.md#quic)

Original row: RFC 9002 (loss detection, PTO, NewReno), split in 0.3.0.

**Requirement.** §6.1: a packet is lost when it is unacknowledged, in flight, sent before an acknowledged packet, and kPacketThreshold packets earlier or kTimeThreshold × max(smoothed_rtt, latest_rtt) earlier. §6.1.1: "Implementations SHOULD NOT use a packet threshold less than 3". §6.1.2: the time threshold is RECOMMENDED 9/8, with a granularity of 1 ms; "If packets sent prior to the largest acknowledged packet cannot yet be declared lost, then a timer SHOULD be set for the remaining time." RFC 9000 §13.1 (with RFC 9002 appendix A.7): an ACK of a packet never sent is a PROTOCOL_VIOLATION.

**Recorded status.** ✅ 0.2.0

**How it is met.**

- `LossDetection.detectLost`: only packets below the largest acknowledged; lost when 3 or more packet numbers below it or sent more than max(9/8 × max(smoothed_rtt, latest_rtt), 1 ms) ago; otherwise the space's loss time is armed at send time + that delay, and `timeout` prefers it to the PTO
- an ACK above the largest packet number sent, or of a skipped packet number (RFC 9000 §21.4), closes the connection with PROTOCOL_VIOLATION
- the ACK walk is sublinear in what is outstanding (RFC 9000 §21.9, [details](9000.md#s21-9-peer-chosen-acks-max-data-and)); a packet declared lost and acknowledged later is reported as a spurious loss (used by BBR's undo) and its data is not resent again
- losses are never ignored (§7.4 lets a sender ignore losses of packets it could not have known were undecryptable; none are)

**Tests that pin it.**

- `recovery.LossDetectionTest.packetThreshold3`, `.timeThreshold9Over8`, `.timeThresholdFloor1ms`, `.negative_ackForUnsentPacket`, `.negative_ackForSkippedPacketNumber`, `.ackOfOldestPacketIsSublinear`, `.ackOfTombstoneRangeIsSublinear`, `.manyRangesAckExactlyTheListedPackets`, `.aLostPacketAcknowledgedLaterIsASpuriousLoss`, `.aTimeThresholdLossAcknowledgedLaterIsSpuriousToo`, `.ackOnlyPacketsNotStored`

## <a id="s6-2-1-computing-the-pto"></a>§6.2.1–§6.2.2 — computing the PTO

Status: ✅ since 0.3.0 · [summary](../rfc-compliance.md#quic)

Original row: RFC 9002 (loss detection, PTO, NewReno), split in 0.3.0.

**Requirement.** §6.2.1: PTO = smoothed_rtt + max(4 × rttvar, kGranularity) + max_ack_delay, max_ack_delay only for the Application Data space; "the timer MUST be set to the earlier value of the Initial and Handshake packet number spaces"; "An endpoint MUST NOT set its PTO timer for the Application Data packet number space until the handshake is confirmed"; "When a PTO timer expires, the PTO backoff MUST be increased" (PTO × 2^pto_count); the backoff is reset on an acknowledgement, except by a client not yet sure the server validated its address. §6.2.2: "When no previous RTT is available, the initial RTT SHOULD be set to 333 milliseconds", and resumed connections or new paths MAY use a previous value.

**Recorded status.** ✅ 0.2.0 (the backoff bound since 0.3.0)

**How it is met.**

- `LossDetection.pto`: smoothed_rtt + max(4 × rttvar, 1 ms), plus max_ack_delay for the Application space once the handshake is confirmed, × 2^pto_count; the earliest of the spaces with ack-eliciting packets in flight, the Application space skipped before confirmation; `onTimeout` increments pto_count; `onAck` resets it when the peer completed address validation (a server always, a client once a Handshake packet is acknowledged or the handshake confirmed)
- the backoff stops doubling at 2^30 (12 days at a 1 ms PTO): 31 unanswered PTOs, possible only with both idle timeouts disabled, used to make the factor negative (the timer fired at once) and 32 restarted it
- `QuicConfig.initialRtt` is 333 ms by default and validated in (0, 10 s]; before the first sample the PTO is 3 × the initial RTT
- the §6.2.2 MAY is taken: a client endpoint starts a new connection to a server address it measured before from that connection's last smoothed RTT (`EndpointCore`'s RTT memory), and a path to a new address starts from the active path's estimate (`QuicConfig.newPathInitialRtt`)

**Tests that pin it.**

- `recovery.LossDetectionTest.initialPto999ms`, `.initialPtoFollowsInitialRtt`, `.appSpacePtoAddsMaxAckDelayAfterConfirmed`, `.ptoBackoffDoubles`, `.ptoBackoffSaturatesInsteadOfWrapping`, `.ptoCountResetOnAck`, `QuicConfigTest.negative_initialRttMustBePositive`, `core.PathTest.validationTimeoutFollowsInitialRtt`

**History.**

- 0.3.0 RFC review: the backoff factor `1 shl pto_count` was unbounded and wrapped at 31 and 32 PTOs

## <a id="s6-2-2-1-before-address-validation"></a>§6.2.2.1 — PTO before address validation

Status: ✅ since 0.3.0 · [summary](../rfc-compliance.md#quic)

Original row: RFC 9002 (loss detection, PTO, NewReno), split in 0.3.0.

**Requirement.** "If no additional data can be sent, the server's PTO timer MUST NOT be armed until datagrams have been received from the client"; "the client MUST set the PTO timer if the client has not received an acknowledgment for any of its Handshake packets and the handshake is not confirmed ..., even if there are no packets in flight. When the PTO fires, the client MUST send a Handshake packet if it has Handshake keys, otherwise it MUST send an Initial packet in a UDP datagram with a payload of at least 1200 bytes."

**Recorded status.** ✅ 0.2.1 (the client case with 0-RTT in flight since 0.3.0)

**How it is met.**

- server: `LossDetection.amplificationBlocked` (`amplificationLeavesNoProbe`) keeps the PTO unarmed while the anti-amplification budget cannot carry a probe (below 64 bytes, or below 1200 bytes with no Handshake data to resend, since an ack-eliciting Initial must fill 1200 bytes); a datagram from the client raises the budget and the timer is evaluated again
- client: with no ack-eliciting packet in flight in the Initial or Handshake space (Application packets count only once the handshake is confirmed, since that space arms no PTO before) and the server not known to have validated the address, the PTO is armed from the last send or acknowledgement in the Handshake space if Handshake keys exist, else the Initial space; the probe is a Handshake packet, or an Initial packet padded to 1200 bytes
- a client whose only ack-eliciting packets in flight were 0-RTT (their acknowledgements come in 1-RTT packets it cannot read yet), after the server acknowledged its Initial, used to arm no timer at all

**Tests that pin it.**

- `recovery.LossDetectionTest.clientAntiDeadlockPtoWithNothingInFlight`, `.clientAntiDeadlockPtoWithOnlyZeroRttInFlight`
- `core.HandshakeLimitsTest.ptoNotArmedWhileAmplificationBlocked`, `.ptoProbesStayWithinAmplificationBudget`, `.negative_noUndersizedAckElicitingServerInitial`

**History.**

- 0.2.1: the server no longer arms a PTO its budget cannot serve
- 0.3.0 RFC review: the anti-deadlock decision counted 0-RTT packets in flight, so a client with only those armed no PTO and the handshake waited for its timeout

## <a id="s6-2-4-sending-probe-packets"></a>§6.2.3–§6.2.4 — probe packets

Status: ✅ since 0.3.0 · [summary](../rfc-compliance.md#quic)

Original row: RFC 9002 (loss detection, PTO, NewReno), split in 0.3.0.

**Requirement.** §6.2.4: "When a PTO timer expires, a sender MUST send at least one ack-eliciting packet in the packet number space as a probe"; "All probe packets sent on a PTO MUST be ack-eliciting"; "the sender SHOULD send ack-eliciting packets from other packet number spaces with in-flight data"; "An endpoint SHOULD include new data in packets that are sent on PTO expiration. Previously sent data MAY be sent if no new data can be sent. Implementations MAY use alternative strategies for determining the content of probe packets"; with nothing to send, "the sender SHOULD send a PING or other ack-eliciting frame". §6.2.3 lets an endpoint resend unacknowledged CRYPTO data before the PTO (MAY).

**Recorded status.** ✅ 0.2.0 (probes of the other spaces since 0.3.0)

**How it is met.**

- `ConnectionCore.runDueTimers` takes `LossDetection.onTimeout`'s `PtoProbe`: one probe when nothing else is in flight, two otherwise (the two-datagram MAY), each ack-eliciting (`PacketBuilder` adds a PING when the packet carries nothing ack-eliciting); a probe is never blocked by the congestion window or the pacer ([§7](#s7-congestion-window))
- other spaces (`probeOtherHandshakeSpace`): a PTO in the Initial or Handshake space also probes the other of the two when it has ack-eliciting packets in flight, and the Application space when it has ack-eliciting packets in flight and 0-RTT or 1-RTT keys (a 0-RTT probe with nothing left to resend is a 0-RTT PING); an Application PTO (only after confirmation) is the only space left
- content, an alternative strategy under the MAY: a probe takes the frames of the oldest unacknowledged packets that hold CRYPTO, STREAM, stream control frames, HANDSHAKE_DONE or NEW_TOKEN and that no earlier probe took (`LossDetection.takeForProbe`, `requeueForProbe`), so successive probes cycle through everything outstanding; new data fills the rest of the probe datagram behind them. Retransmission comes first because the probes exist to repair what a lost flight carried (a handshake whose HANDSHAKE_DONE and response were lost once stalled for 30 s of PING-only probes); resent STREAM data never carries a FIN still in flight
- the PTO does not declare anything lost (the §6.2.4 MAY to mark packets lost instead of probing is not taken)
- §6.2.3 (MAY) is not used: CRYPTO data is resent on the PTO only; a server that mirrors its handshake to a rebinding client's second address does that on its PTO too

**Tests that pin it.**

- `core.PtoProbeSpacesTest.aHandshakePtoAlsoProbesApplicationDataInFlight`, `.anInitialPtoAlsoProbesZeroRttDataInFlight`, `core.HandshakeTest.serverInitialPtoAlsoProbesHandshakeSpace`
- `core.PtoProbeContentTest.probesResendDataBehindPingOnlyPackets`, `stream.SendStreamTest.retransmittedDataDoesNotCarryAFinThatIsStillInFlight`
- `core.CongestionWindowTest.thePacerDoesNotHoldAProbeBack`

**History.**

- 0.2.0 release gate (`kuicksilver->ngtcp2:handshakeloss`): a probe took the two oldest packets whatever they held; with a PING and NEW_CONNECTION_ID frames there and every client ACK lost, 30 s of probes were ACK + PING while HANDSHAKE_DONE and the response were never resent
- 0.3.0 RFC review: a Handshake or Initial PTO probed only the other long-header space; Application data in flight (a client's first 1-RTT data behind its Finished, or 0-RTT data) went with the probe only when new data happened to be queued

**Peer interop notes.**

- resent STREAM data carries no FIN still in flight: quiche's receiver takes the final size from a frame whose bytes it already read and never wakes the application for the FIN (`kuicksilver->quiche:handshakeloss` lost one request in 50, run 20261015-170940)

## <a id="s6-3-retry-and-discarding-keys"></a>§6.3, §6.4 — Retry and discarding keys

Status: ✅ since 0.3.0 · [summary](../rfc-compliance.md#quic)

Original row: RFC 9002 (loss detection, PTO, NewReno), split in 0.3.0.

**Requirement.** §6.3: on a Retry the client's Initial packets leave flight without a congestion signal, their data is sent again, and the PTO backoff is reset. §6.4: "When Initial or Handshake keys are discarded, the Initial or Handshake packets ... are removed from bytes_in_flight", "loss detection state ... is reset"; "When 0-RTT is rejected, recovery state for all in-flight 0-RTT packets is discarded."

**Recorded status.** ✅ 0.2.0

**How it is met.**

- `LossDetection.discard`: the space's packets leave bytes in flight, its loss time, last ack-eliciting send time and ack-eliciting count are cleared, the remembered losses settle, pto_count is reset; `discardZeroRtt` does the same for 0-RTT packets (a rejection, a Retry, a Version Negotiation restart) after queueing their frames again
- the bytes leave the congestion controller that counted each packet (`LossListener.onDiscarded` with the packet's controller): after a migration or rebinding that is not necessarily the active path's
- a PTO probe still owed in the space is dropped with it (`discardSpace` and a Retry clear `probePending`), and a probe count exempts a datagram from the window only while its space is live and has write keys
- Retry: the client's Initial CRYPTO data is queued again, its Initial packets and 0-RTT packets are discarded as above

**Tests that pin it.**

- `recovery.LossDetectionTest.discardSpaceRemovesBytesInFlight`, `.discardingASpaceSettlesItsLostPackets`, `.discardedBytesLeaveTheControllerThatCountedThem`
- `core.CongestionWindowTest.aProbeLeftInADiscardedSpaceDoesNotLiftTheWindow`, `core.ReceivePathTest.initialKeysDiscardedOnFirstHandshakePacket`, `core.ZeroRttTest.zeroRttRejectedResetsStreams`, `endpoint.AdmissionTest.alwaysRetryUsedByInteropRetryCase`

**History.**

- 0.3.0 RFC review: a probe owed in a space that was then discarded (a server's Initial probe its anti-amplification budget held back, then the client's Handshake packet) was never lowered, and every later datagram counted as a PTO probe: the window was off for the rest of the connection
- 0.3.0 RFC review: discarded bytes were taken from the active path's controller whichever controller had counted them

## <a id="s7-congestion-window"></a>§7, §7.2–§7.5 — the congestion window

Status: ✅ since 0.3.0 · [summary](../rfc-compliance.md#quic)

Original row: RFC 9002 (loss detection, PTO, NewReno), split in 0.3.0.

**Requirement.** §7: "An endpoint MUST NOT send a packet if it would cause bytes_in_flight (see Appendix B.2) to be larger than the congestion window, unless the packet is sent on a PTO timer expiration (see Section 6.2) or when entering recovery"; §2: a packet is in flight when it is ack-eliciting or carries PADDING; RFC 9000 §14.4: "PMTU probes consume congestion window", while their loss "SHOULD NOT trigger a congestion control reaction". §7.2: "Endpoints SHOULD use an initial congestion window of ten times the maximum datagram size ..., while limiting the window to the larger of 14,720 bytes or twice the maximum datagram size"; "If the maximum datagram size changes during the connection, the initial congestion window SHOULD be recalculated with the new size. If the maximum datagram size is decreased ..., the congestion window SHOULD be set to the new initial congestion window"; the minimum window is RECOMMENDED 2 × max_datagram_size. §7.3: slow start, recovery and congestion avoidance (appendix B). §7.5: "Probe packets MUST NOT be blocked by the congestion controller."

**Recorded status.** ✅ 0.2.0 (the literal window check, PMTU probes and the initial-window update since 0.3.0)

**How it is met.**

- `PacketBuilder.buildDatagram`: a datagram is built only while bytes in flight plus its largest possible size (this poll's datagram limit) fit in the window, so bytes in flight never exceed it. With less room than that but at least 1200 bytes (what any packet may need, RFC 9000 §14.1) the datagram is cut to the room, so a window smaller than a jumbo MTU still carries data; a window counts as full with less than min(one datagram, 1200 bytes) left. Exempt: a datagram while a PTO probe is owed in a live space with keys (neither the window nor the pacer holds it), ACK-only packets (not in flight), and PATH_CHALLENGE / PATH_RESPONSE (RFC 9000 §9.1, [details](9000.md#s9-1-path-challenge-outside-the-congestion-window))
- a DPLPMTUD probe counts in flight and goes only when its size fits in the window and the pacer allows (`Pmtud.deferProbe` keeps it for the next send opportunity); a lost probe leaves flight through the controller's `onDiscarded`, with no congestion event, no part in persistent congestion and no BBR loss
- initial window min(10 × MDS, max(14 720, 2 × MDS)) in `NewReno` and `Cubic` (BBR raises it to its 4-datagram floor); on a datagram size change the minimum window follows, and a window still at its initial value (slow start, never grown or reduced) becomes the new size's initial window when the size shrinks. The update itself never raises the window in bytes (RFC 8899 §3 item 7, "An update to the PLPMTU (or MPS) MUST NOT increase the congestion window measured in bytes"); that binds the update only, so a later congestion event reduces as any other, to max(reduced, 2 × the new MDS) for ssthresh and cwnd (§7.3.2, appendix B.6), and persistent congestion to 2 × the new MDS
- NewReno (§7.3, appendix B): slow start adds the acknowledged bytes; congestion avoidance adds MDS × acked / cwnd with the remainder carried (whole bytes stopped growing above 1.44 MB); one halving per recovery period, judged by the send time of the largest lost packet against the start of recovery; no growth from packets sent before recovery began; minimum window 2 × MDS. CUBIC: [RFC 9438](9438.md#cubic)
- §7.1 ECN: a CE increase is a congestion event, once per recovery period ([details](9000.md#s13-4-ecn))
- §7.4: losses are never ignored

**Tests that pin it.**

- `core.CongestionWindowTest.noDatagramTakesBytesInFlightAboveTheWindow`, `.aProbeLeftInADiscardedSpaceDoesNotLiftTheWindow`, `.thePacerDoesNotHoldAProbeBack`, `.aPmtuProbeCountsInFlightAndWaitsForTheWindow`, `.aLostPmtuProbeLeavesFlightWithoutACongestionEvent`, `.aWindowSmallerThanTheMtuStillCarriesData`, `core.PmtudTest.probeLossNotCongestion`
- `recovery.NewRenoTest.*` (`initialWindow12000At1200`, `initialWindowFor1452`, `minimumWindow2400`, `slowStartGrowsByAckedBytes`, `lossHalvesWindow`, `noSecondReductionInsideRecovery`, `congestionAvoidanceOneDatagramPerWindow`, `congestionAvoidanceKeepsGrowingAboveMssSquared`, `ecnCeIsCongestionEvent`, `theInitialWindowFollowsTheDatagramSize`, `aReductionAfterTheDatagramSizeGrewLandsOnTheNewMinimumWindow`), `recovery.CubicTest.initialWindowLikeNewReno`, `core.EcnTest.ceMarkTriggersOneCongestionEventPerRecovery`

**History.**

- 0.2.0: NewReno's congestion avoidance carries the remainder of MDS × acked / cwnd
- 0.3.0 RFC review: the check stopped only once bytes in flight reached the window, so a datagram could take them up to one MTU above it; DPLPMTUD probes bypassed the window and never counted in flight (a comment cited RFC 9000 §14.4 for that, which says the opposite); only the minimum window followed a new datagram size

## <a id="s7-6-persistent-congestion"></a>§7.6 — persistent congestion

Status: ✅ since 0.3.0 · [summary](../rfc-compliance.md#quic)

Original row: RFC 9002 (loss detection, PTO, NewReno), split in 0.3.0.

**Requirement.** §7.6.1: the duration is (smoothed_rtt + max(4 × rttvar, kGranularity) + max_ack_delay) × kPersistentCongestionThreshold (3). §7.6.2: persistent congestion needs two ack-eliciting packets declared lost such that "across all packet number spaces, none of the packets sent between the send times of these two packets are acknowledged", more than the duration apart, and "a prior RTT sample existed when these two packets were sent"; "The persistent congestion period SHOULD NOT start until there is at least one RTT sample"; "persistent congestion SHOULD consider packets sent across packet number spaces"; "When persistent congestion is declared, the sender's congestion window MUST be reduced to the minimum congestion window (kMinimumWindow)".

**Recorded status.** ✅ 0.2.0 (across spaces, per-path first sample and BBR's collapse since 0.3.0)

**How it is met.**

- `LossDetection.persistentCongestion`, for the lost packets of one detection (as appendix A.9), grouped by the path and controller that counted them (RFC 9000 §9.4): a run of lost ack-eliciting in-flight packets, broken by an acknowledged packet sent between two of them, longer than `persistentCongestionDuration` of that path's own RTT estimator (`LossListener.rttOf`; a path that is gone declares none), not the active path's
- "between" in the same space by packet number (each space keeps the acknowledged ranges above its oldest outstanding packet, so ACK-only and skipped numbers do not break a run; padding-only packets cannot end one); in the other spaces by send time: before the handshake is confirmed each space keeps the send times of its newly acknowledged packets (the newest 16 per space; an older forgotten one is assumed to lie in between, which can only withhold a declaration). The check runs at each ACK frame with the acknowledgements processed until then, as appendix A.7 orders it ("after the receipt of an acknowledgment"): when one datagram coalesces ACK frames of several spaces, an acknowledgement in a later packet of the datagram is not known yet when an earlier packet's ACK frame declares losses, so a period it would break may already be established (the datagram's packets are processed in order, each completely, RFC 9000 §12.2). A space's times go when the space is discarded, the Application space's once Initial and Handshake are both gone: from then on the MAY for a sender "that does not have state for all packet number spaces" applies, and after confirmation only the Application space is left
- only packets sent at or after the mark of the path's own RTT estimator count: its first sample, or its newest sample once that path established persistent congestion (so a lasting RTT increase is not declared again and again, as ngtcp2's first_rtt_sample_ts); a new path's packets wait for that path's first sample
- the response: NewReno and CUBIC collapse to 2 × MDS and leave recovery; BBR collapses to BBR.MinPipeCwnd (4 datagrams), its own minimum window ([details](draft-ietf-ccwg-bbr.md#bbr-bbrv3)); min_rtt of that path's estimator restarts from its newest sample (§5.2)
- a lost DPLPMTUD probe is no part of a run (RFC 9000 §14.4)

**Tests that pin it.**

- `recovery.LossDetectionTest.persistentCongestionDuration975ms`, `.persistentCongestionDetected`, `.persistentCongestionIgnoresPacketsSentBeforeTheFirstRttSample`, `.persistentCongestionSpansAckOnlyAndSkippedPacketNumbers`, `.anAcknowledgedPacketBetweenTheLostOnesBreaksPersistentCongestion`, `.aPacketAcknowledgedInAnotherSpaceBreaksPersistentCongestion`, `.persistentCongestionSpanIsBetweenAckElicitingPackets`, `.persistentCongestionIsJudgedPerPath`, `.persistentCongestionWaitsForTheNewPathsFirstRttSample`, `.persistentCongestionOfAnotherPathIsJudgedByThatPathsRtt`, `.persistentCongestionResetsMinRttToTheNewestSample`
- `recovery.NewRenoTest.persistentCongestionCollapsesToMinimum`, `recovery.CubicTest.persistentCongestionToMinimum`, `recovery.BbrTest.persistentCongestionCollapsesToTheMinimumWindow`

**History.**

- 0.2.0 review: persistent congestion between two lost ack-eliciting packets, both sent after the first RTT sample (a lossy handshake used to collapse to the minimum window), broken only by a packet acknowledged between them, judged per path
- 0.3.0 RFC review: only the space whose losses were judged could break a run; the first-sample time was per connection, so after a migration the old path's sample counted for the new path's packets, and another path's losses were judged by the active path's RTT; BBR kept inflight + 1 datagram (`OnEnterRTO`) instead of collapsing to its minimum window

## <a id="s7-7-pacing"></a>§7.7 — pacing and bursts

Status: ✅ since 0.3.0 · [summary](../rfc-compliance.md#quic)

Original row: RFC 9002 (loss detection, PTO, NewReno), split in 0.3.0.

**Requirement.** "A sender SHOULD pace sending of all in-flight packets based on input from the congestion controller"; "Senders MUST either use pacing or limit such bursts. Senders SHOULD limit bursts to the initial congestion window"; "packets containing only ACK frames SHOULD therefore not be paced"; the rate N × cwnd / smoothed_rtt with N small but at least 1 (1.25 suggested).

**Recorded status.** ✅ 0.2.0 (pacing below 1 ms of RTT and the initial-window burst since 0.3.0)

**How it is met.**

- `Pacer`, a token bucket per path at the controller's `pacingRate` (NewReno and CUBIC 1.25 × cwnd / smoothed_rtt), at every smoothed RTT; the bucket holds `sendQuantum`, which for NewReno and CUBIC is the initial window (at most 64 KiB unless one datagram is larger), and a poll sends at most one quantum before it asks to be polled again when the pacer allows
- every in-flight packet spends credit; ACK-only packets are not paced; PTO probes are not held by the pacer (§7.5)
- below 1 ms of smoothed RTT the rate refills a burst within microseconds, so the window, not the pacer, stays the limit there
- BBR's pacing rate is its own (draft-ietf-ccwg-bbr §5.6.2); its burst is the draft's send quantum, max(min(1 ms of the rate, 64 KiB), 2 datagrams), capped at the initial window (0.3.0: above about 15 MB/s the quantum used to exceed it). The draft's quantum still sizes BBR's quantization budget (§5.6.4.2)
- measured (`UdpThroughputBenchmark`, JVM, loopback, Null crypto, macOS, 2026-11-11, three back-to-back pairs of 5 × 2 s): BBR 225–231 MiB/s with the uncapped quantum, 208–214 MiB/s capped at the initial window, as CUBIC (212–214 MiB/s, bursts of the initial window); with the loop's timer coroutine yielding instead of sleeping when its next deadline was under 1 ms away (the sleep rounds such waits up to 1 ms) the capped BBR gave 210 MiB/s, so the difference is the smaller burst per send, not the timer's granularity. The 1 Gbit/s × 0.2 ms BBR simulation (`testing.BbrHighRateScenarioTest`) keeps 965 Mbit/s

**Tests that pin it.**

- `recovery.PacerTest.*` (`rateIs125PercentOfCwndPerSrtt`, `burstUpToTenDatagrams`, `windowControllersArePacedBelow1msSrtt`, `windowControllersBurstAtMostTheInitialWindow`, `bucketRefillsAfterALongGap`, `sendQuantumCapped`, `aLongIdleGapAtTenGbitPerSecondRefillsTheBucket`, `cubicAfterALongIdleGapSendsAtOnce`, `bbrIsPacedEvenBelow1msSrtt`, `negative_windowControllersPaceAWindowAbove7GB`), `recovery.BbrTest.theBurstStaysWithinTheInitialWindowAtAHighRate`, `core.SendContinuationTest.quantumLimitedPollArmsTheNextSend`

**History.**

- 0.3.0 cross-review: NewReno's and CUBIC's rate, 1.25 × cwnd × 10⁹ / sRTT in `Long` arithmetic, overflowed above a window of about 7.4 GB and came out negative; the pacer took that as 1 byte a second and held the next datagram for hours. Above that window the rate is computed in `Double` and saturates, and the pacer caps any controller's rate at 2⁶² bytes a second so its refill arithmetic cannot overflow
- 0.3.0 RFC review: NewReno and CUBIC were not paced below 1 ms of smoothed RTT, and a poll that had sent a quantum asked to be polled again at once, so a whole window could leave back to back; their quantum, 10 datagrams, exceeded the initial window above 1472-byte datagrams; BBR's burst exceeded it above about 15 MB/s

## <a id="s7-8-underutilizing-the-congestion-window"></a>§7.8 — underutilizing the congestion window

Status: ✅ since 0.3.0 · [summary](../rfc-compliance.md#quic)

Original row: RFC 9002 (loss detection, PTO, NewReno), split in 0.3.0.

**Requirement.** "When bytes in flight is smaller than the congestion window and sending is not pacing limited, the congestion window is underutilized ... When this occurs, the congestion window SHOULD NOT be increased in either slow start or congestion avoidance"; "A sender SHOULD NOT consider itself application limited if it would have fully utilized the congestion window without pacing delay"; alternatives such as RFC 7661 MAY be used.

**Recorded status.** ✅ 0.2.0 (the literal rule since 0.3.0)

**How it is met.**

- `ConnectionLossListener.cwndLimited`, per acknowledged packet: the window was used when the bytes in flight before the ACK frame left no room for another datagram in the window before the frame (taken at the controller's first event of the frame, so an ACK of a whole window does not look under-used as the window grows with it; §7 lets a window be used to within one datagram), or when the active path's sender ended its last poll with data still to send (`PacketBuilder.appLimited` false): it stopped for the window, the pacer, the send quantum or a full batch, not for want of data
- running out of data, or of flow-control credit, with room in the window is application-limited, and the controller's `onAck` gets `appLimited`; another path's controller sends no data and is judged by its bytes alone
- NewReno and CUBIC do not grow on an application-limited ACK; BBR (draft-ietf-ccwg-bbr §5.6.4.6 SetCwnd adds every ACK's delivered bytes, below BBR.max_inflight, whatever was in flight) adds nothing for an ACK frame none of whose ACKs was cwnd-limited, and may still lower the window to BBR.max_inflight and the model's bounds (0.3.0); its bandwidth model keeps the draft's own treatment of application-limited samples
- the RFC 7661 MAY is not used

**Tests that pin it.**

- `core.AppLimitedTest.aFullWindowAcknowledgedInOneFrameGrowsByAllOfIt`, `.congestionAvoidanceDoesNotGrowOnAFifthOfTheWindowUnused`, `.twoDatagramsOfTheWindowUnusedDoNotGrowIt`, `.slowStartWithAThirdOfTheWindowUnusedDoesNotGrow`, `.aSenderHeldBackByThePacerIsNotApplicationLimited`, `recovery.NewRenoTest.appLimitedDoesNotGrow`, `recovery.CubicTest.appLimitedDoesNotGrow`, `recovery.BbrTest.applicationLimitedAcksDoNotGrowTheWindow`

**History.**

- 0.2.0 review: the window grew only when the sender used it before the ACK frame, with quic-go's isCwndLimited margins (at most 3 datagrams unused, or more than half in flight in slow start)
- 0.3.0 RFC review: those margins grew windows the sender did not use; replaced by the literal rule, with pacing-limited sending counted as use
- 0.3.0 cross-review: BBR ignored `appLimited` and grew its window on every ACK as draft-06 does (a trickle of one datagram per RTT took it from 12 000 to 24 000 bytes in Startup), although this file claims every row for all three controllers
