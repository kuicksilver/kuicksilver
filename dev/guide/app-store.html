Publishing an app that uses Kuicksilver (App Store, Mac App Store, notarized macOS)

What this page is for. It tells the owner of an iOS, Mac App Store or notarized macOS app that embeds Kuicksilver what to answer Apple (export compliance, privacy manifest, App Review) and what to file with the U.S. export authority, with templates to copy.

Before you start

  • Your app links Kuicksilver: the klibs io.kuicksilver:kuicksilver-http3, -quic or -ktor-client (version 0.3.0 or newer), or Kuicksilver.xcframework through the Swift package kuicksilver-swift (from 0.3.0; iOS: Choosing how to consume it).
  • Minimum OS versions: iOS 15; macOS 12 (Kotlin/Native) or macOS 11 (JVM) (Platforms).
  • You can open your app in App Store Connect and edit its Info.plist.

How to use this page. This is a procedure, not an API: work through sections 1 to 6 in order, then copy what you need from Templates. The iOS build itself is in iOS.

This page walks through what Apple and the U.S. export rules ask of an app that embeds Kuicksilver, and ends with templates you can copy and fill in. The iOS build itself (frameworks, privacy manifest, size, device checks) is in ios.md.

Not legal advice. Export classification and the answers you give Apple are the app owner's decision and responsibility. This page summarises the sources as of 2026-10-13 and links them; check them, and ask counsel when the stakes are real. The project cannot classify your app for you.

Sources used: Apple, Complying with encryption export regulations, Overview of export compliance, Export compliance documentation for encryption, ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption, the App Store Connect API AppEncryptionDeclaration attributes (the same questions the web form asks), required reason API; the EAR as published in the eCFR: 15 CFR 740.17 (License Exception ENC) and Supplement No. 8 to Part 742 (self-classification report); BIS, encryption controls.

1. Export compliance

Why Kuicksilver counts as non-exempt encryption. Apple treats encryption that is part of its operating systems (HTTPS through URLSession, for example) as typically exempt from export documentation, and other encryption as not. Kuicksilver ships its own TLS 1.3 (OpenSSL 3.5, statically linked) to secure QUIC, so an app that links it "implements standard encryption algorithms instead of, or in addition to, using or accessing the encryption in Apple's operating systems". Only standard, published algorithms are used (no proprietary cryptography).

ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption. Without this Info.plist key, App Store Connect asks the encryption questions for every build you upload (TestFlight builds show "Missing Compliance" until answered). With Kuicksilver linked, set it to true. false means "no encryption, or only exempt encryption", which is not the case for a Kuicksilver app even if you only use it as a client. If App Store Connect issues a code after reviewing uploaded documentation, add it as ITSEncryptionExportComplianceCode.

What App Store Connect asks. The questionnaire records, in order (the field names are those of the API):

  1. Does the app use, contain or incorporate cryptography? (usesEncryption)
  2. Is it exempt based on its use of encryption and availability? (exempt)
  3. Does it implement proprietary encryption algorithms? (containsProprietaryCryptography)
  4. Does it implement standard encryption algorithms instead of, or in addition to, the encryption in Apple's operating systems? (containsThirdPartyCryptography)
  5. Will it be distributed on the App Store in France? (availableOnFrenchStore)

Apple's documentation table then says what to upload: encryption limited to the operating system needs nothing; an "industry standard algorithm, not provided within the Apple operating system" needs the French encryption declaration, and only if you distribute in France; proprietary algorithms additionally need a CCATS from BIS (not the case with Kuicksilver). The French declaration is filed with ANSSI; App Store Connect takes the resulting document under App Information > App Encryption Documentation, and Apple says it reviews it in about two business days.

The usual U.S. path. An app that uses standard cryptography to secure its own communication and is sold or given away to the general public is normally a "mass market" item: it is exported under License Exception ENC, 15 CFR 740.17(b)(1), and after self-classification is classified 5D992.c (mass-market software), "removed from EI and NS controls". The annual self-classification report (740.17(e)(3), format in Supplement No. 8 to Part 742) applies to mass-market components and certain "executable software" and to items that stay in 5x002; a finished mass-market app is outside it, but the recordkeeping of part 762 applies (keep your classification reasoning). If you ship a component (for example an SDK built on Kuicksilver for other developers), the report can apply to you: see the template below. ENC never covers the embargoed destinations of Country Groups E:1/E:2.

Mac App Store uses the same App Store Connect questions and Info.plist keys. Notarized apps outside the store see no questionnaire, but the EAR applies to the export the same way.

2. Why the app uses its own cryptography

App Review or a compliance contact may ask why the app does not use Apple's encryption. The answer is technical and short: QUIC needs a TLS 1.3 stack with an interface that Apple's TLS APIs do not offer. The texts in Templates are ready to paste.

3. Privacy manifest

Ship kuicksilver-native/apple/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy or merge its entries into your app's manifest (the Maven artifacts do not carry the file; template 6 below is the same manifest, ready to paste) (ios.md, Privacy manifest explains the audit): no tracking, no collected data, and one required-reason category, file timestamp APIs with reason C617.1 (OpenSSL's file loaders and the key-log file check call stat/fstat/lstat on files inside the app container). Apple requires manifests on iOS, iPadOS, tvOS, visionOS and watchOS; a macOS app may carry the same file. Re-run tools/check/ios-private-api.py on your Release binary when you upgrade: it prints the required-reason APIs it finds (a single file; ios.md, Checks says how to run it without the repository).

4. Info.plist and entitlements

Key When Value
ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption always with Kuicksilver true
ITSEncryptionExportComplianceCode only if App Store Connect gave you a code the code
NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription the app talks to LAN devices (a server on the phone, LAN peers, discovery); iOS 14+, macOS 15+ why, in the user's words
NSBonjourServices the app advertises or browses Bonjour services e.g. _myservice._udp
com.apple.security.network.client (macOS App Sandbox) any client: QUIC, HTTP/3, TLS true
com.apple.security.network.server (macOS App Sandbox) the app accepts connections: a QUIC endpoint with server, the Ktor server engine true
  • iOS apps have no network entitlements; App Transport Security does not apply to Kuicksilver's own sockets.
  • Hardened runtime and notarization (macOS): no exception entitlements are needed. Kotlin/Native compiles ahead of time and allocates ordinary read-write memory; OpenSSL is built without dynamic engines or modules. Checked on 2026-10-13 by signing a Kotlin/Native test binary of kuicksilver-tls with codesign --options runtime and no entitlements and running its handshake tests (11 passed). Do not add com.apple.security.cs.allow-jit, allow-unsigned-executable-memory or disable-library-validation for Kuicksilver.
  • With the App Sandbox, test the signed, sandboxed build: a missing network.server shows up only when a peer connects.

5. Do you need your own crypto at all?

If the app only needs an HTTP client, Ktor's Darwin engine runs on URLSession, which negotiates HTTP/3 by itself when the server advertises it (iOS 15 and macOS 12 or later) and uses the operating system's encryption: no non-exempt encryption, no French declaration, no OpenSSL in the binary. Choose Kuicksilver when you need what URLSession does not give you:

  • a raw QUIC API: your own protocol over streams, QUIC datagrams (RFC 9221), stream priorities, connection migration under your control;
  • a server (HTTP/3, QUIC, or TLS over TCP) inside the app;
  • control over 0-RTT (opt-in, replay-safe methods only), TLS policy (pins, a private CA, TOFU) and transport settings;
  • the same protocol stack and behaviour on Android, the JVM, Linux and Windows.

6. App Review

  • IPv6-only networks. Apple reviews apps on an IPv6-only network with NAT64/DNS64 and rejects apps that fail there. Bind ::, connect by host name, never ship IPv4 literals. Test it with macOS Internet Sharing's NAT64 option (ios.md, Device checklist, item 5).
  • QUIC uses UDP, usually to port 443. Networks that block UDP fall back to nothing unless your app has a TCP path; say in the review notes what the reviewer should see if UDP is blocked.
  • Checklist before you submit:
    • ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption = true; the questionnaire answered once; French declaration uploaded if you distribute in France.
    • Privacy manifest included; ios-private-api.py on the Release binary reports no non-public symbols and no required-reason API missing from the manifest.
    • Another OpenSSL or BoringSSL in the app: nothing to do, Kuicksilver's OpenSSL symbols are renamed (the project's tools/check/ios-symbols.sh proves it for each release, ios.md, Checks).
    • Size impact measured from your app's linker map (the project's measurement, on the 0.2.0 code: about 3.4 MB for OpenSSL + Kuicksilver, about 5.6 MB with the Kotlin runtime in an app that has no other Kotlin; ios.md, Size).
    • Tested on a device, Wi-Fi and cellular, and on NAT64.
    • NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription/NSBonjourServices present if and only if the app uses the LAN.
    • Review notes filled in (template below), with a test server the reviewer can reach without an account.

3. Why non-Apple encryption

Long version (paste and adjust):

by uses QUIC (IETF RFC 9000 and RFC 9001) and HTTP/3 to . QUIC builds its security on TLS 1.3 in a way that requires direct access to the TLS handshake: the QUIC implementation must receive the handshake messages for each encryption level separately, install the traffic secrets for each level itself, and carry QUIC transport parameters inside the TLS handshake. Apple's TLS interfaces (Security and Network frameworks) do not expose these steps to a third-party QUIC implementation, so the app includes the Kuicksilver library, which uses OpenSSL 3.5 as its TLS 1.3 implementation. Only standard, published algorithms are used: AES-GCM and ChaCha20-Poly1305 for encryption, X25519 and the X25519/ML-KEM hybrid for key exchange, ECDSA and RSA signature verification, and HKDF with SHA-2. The cryptography is used only to authenticate the servers the app connects to and to encrypt the app's own network traffic; it is not offered to users or other apps as a general-purpose encryption function. Server certificates are verified with Apple's trust evaluation (SecTrust) and the system trust store. The app contains no proprietary or non-standard cryptography.

One line:

implements QUIC/HTTP-3, which needs a TLS 1.3 stack Apple's APIs do not expose, so it embeds OpenSSL (standard algorithms only) solely to authenticate servers and encrypt its own network traffic.

If the app also runs a server, add: "The app can also accept connections from on the local network, secured the same way."

5. Annual self-classification report (only if it applies)

A finished mass-market app is normally outside 740.17(e)(3) (see section 1); the report is for mass-market components and certain executable software classified 5A992/5D992, and for items that stay in 5A002/5D002 after self-classification, exported under 740.17(b)(1). Verify with your counsel whether it applies to you. Format: Supplement No. 8 to Part 742, a .csv file whose first line is exactly the twelve headers below, one row per product, no empty cells, company columns repeated on every row.

PRODUCT NAME,MODEL NUMBER,MANUFACTURER,ECCN,AUTHORIZATION TYPE,ITEM TYPE,SUBMITTER NAME,TELEPHONE NUMBER,E-MAIL ADDRESS,MAILING ADDRESS,NON-U.S. COMPONENTS,NON-U.S. MANUFACTURING LOCATIONS
<PRODUCT NAME>,<VERSION OR N/A>,SELF,5D992,MMKT,Mobility and mobile applications n.e.s.,<COMPANY>,<PHONE>,<EMAIL>,<ADDRESS>,<YES/NO>,<CITY COUNTRY OR NONE>

AUTHORIZATION TYPE is MMKT for mass market or ENC; ITEM TYPE comes from the list in the supplement (for an SDK, "Other (please specify)" with a short description may fit better). Send it for each calendar year by February 1 of the next year, by email to crypt-supp8@bis.doc.gov and enc@nsa.gov, subject "self-classification report", stating the period covered and a contact. A product is reported once; in a year with no new products, an email saying nothing has changed is sent instead.
